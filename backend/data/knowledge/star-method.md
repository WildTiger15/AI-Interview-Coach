# The STAR Method

STAR is the standard structure for answering behavioral interview questions
("Tell me about a time when..."). It keeps answers concrete and focused on
your actual contribution instead of vague generalities.

- **Situation** — Briefly set the scene. What was the context? One or two
  sentences is enough; don't over-explain the backstory.
- **Task** — What was your specific responsibility or goal in that situation?
  What were you trying to achieve, and why did it matter?
- **Action** — What did *you* actually do? This is the most important part of
  the answer and should be the longest. Use "I", not "we", when describing
  your own contribution. Be specific about decisions you made and why.
- **Result** — What happened? Quantify it when you can (%, $, time saved,
  users affected). If the result was a failure, say what you learned and how
  you applied it afterward — interviewers value self-awareness as much as
  success.

## Common mistakes with STAR
- Spending 80% of the answer on Situation/Task and rushing the Action.
- Describing what the *team* did instead of your own specific role.
- Skipping the Result entirely, or leaving it vague ("it went well").
- Picking an example that doesn't actually match what was asked.
- Rambling without a clear endpoint — a STAR answer should be 60–90 seconds
  spoken, not five minutes.

## Quick template
"In [situation], I was responsible for [task]. I decided to [key action(s)],
because [reasoning]. As a result, [quantified outcome], and I learned
[takeaway]."
